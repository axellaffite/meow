# A 3D creature

Meow can load, animate and draw a skinned 3D model with Metal, in a
transparent window like the cat's. Nothing in the app uses it yet: the cat is
still drawn by its styles. This page says what the engine does, how to bring
a model, and what wiring it into the cat would take.

| Module            | What it does                                                        |
|-------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------|
| `creature`        | Reads a GLB: meshes, skeleton, sockets, clips, custom properties; poses |
| `motion`          | Plays clips: crossfades, overlays through a bone mask, springs; one `Frame` a tick |
| `render`          | Draws a `Frame` with Metal: shaders, offscreen rendering, pacing, hit test |
| `ui::metal_view`  | The `CAMetalLayer` in a window, Low Power Mode, click-through       |

Try it:

```sh
cargo run --example model_preview -- out.png              # 8 frames on light and dark, no window
cargo run --example model_preview -- creature.glb out.png # your own model (alone, it writes model.png)
cargo run --example model_window -- creature.glb          # on the desktop; click it to nod
```

Both default to `tests/fixtures/mini_creature.glb`, a box with two eyes, four
bones and two clips, made by `tools/blender/make_fixture.py`.

## Bringing a creature

One GLB, exported from Blender, read by `CreatureAsset::load`:

- **Space**: meters, Y up, the creature facing +Z (toward the viewer), its
  left side toward +X, its origin on the ground. In Blender: Z up, facing -Y;
  the exporter converts. Nothing is converted at load.
- **One armature, one skin**. Every buffer in the GLB's binary chunk,
  triangles only. A malformed file is an error, never a panic.
- **Meshes** are skinned to the armature, or parented to one bone (an eye,
  a prop): such a mesh is skinned whole to that bone at load, and follows
  it. One parented to nothing stays where it is.
- **`ctrl_*` bones** (optional) are data, not geometry: non-deform bones whose
  translation a clip uses as numbers (an expression, a mode). Keyed CONSTANT
  in Blender (glTF `STEP`), they are discrete: never averaged in a
  crossfade, taken whole from the heavier clip. `Animator::set_discrete`
  marks one when the export baked its steps to linear keys.
- **`socket_*` empties**, parented to a bone, mark where things attach
  (`CreatureAsset::socket`; each tick, `pose::socket_in_window` with
  `Animator::world` and `LiveView::to_window`). A `_L` / `_R` suffix
  pairs them: `socket_mirrored` swaps the sides when the creature is drawn
  facing the other way.
- **Clips** are Blender actions, played by name (`idle`, `walk`…), each
  keying every bone at its first and last frame so none leaks into the next.
- **Materials**: the Principled BSDF's base colour, roughness, metallic and
  emission, and backface culling (glTF `doubleSided`). `COLOR_0`, when the
  mesh has one, multiplies the base colour. No textures yet.
- **Custom properties** come through as JSON: `asset.extras` by node name,
  `asset.scene_extras`, and each joint's and socket's `extras`.

### Export settings

The exporter settings the loader expects are those of
`tools/blender/make_fixture.py` (`export`), which refuses to run when a
Blender version renames one. The ones that matter: `export_yup`,
`export_def_bones: False` (the `ctrl_*` bones stay), `export_extras`,
`export_animation_mode: "ACTIONS"`, `export_anim_slide_to_zero`, and
`export_vertex_color: "ACTIVE"`. Headless, tested with Blender 5.2:

```sh
blender -b --factory-startup -P tools/blender/make_fixture.py -- tests/fixtures/mini_creature.glb
```

## Playing clips

```rust
let mut animator = Animator::new(&asset);
animator.play("wave", Play::Once, 1.0, 0.15); // crossfade over 0.15 s
animator.play("idle", Play::Loop, 1.0, 0.25); // waits for the wave to end
let mut frame = animator.update(dt, &placement);
// each tick, reusing the frame's buffers:
animator.update_into(dt, &placement, &mut frame);
```

- A new clip asked for while a `Once` clip plays fades in as that one ends.
- `overlay(name, play, fade, mask)` plays over the base on the bones a mask
  weighs (`Mixer::mask_below(skeleton, head)`: the head and all under it).
- Procedural layers (a head that follows the pointer, a tail on a spring)
  go between `advance(dt)` and `write_frame`, on `pose_mut()`.
  `motion::spring::Spring` is solved exactly, and `motion::rig` turns a
  wish in creature axes into a bone's local transform.
- Everything runs on seconds: a change starts at the end of the frame it
  comes in, springs step at most 1/240 s, so 30 and 120 fps move alike.
- A steady tick allocates nothing (`tests/live_loop_allocations.rs`).
- `Placement` sets where it stands in its window (points), how big it is
  (points per meter), whether it faces the other way, and a turn.

## Drawing

- `Pending::spawn(asset)` compiles the shaders on a worker thread (no Xcode
  needed: they are compiled from source at runtime); `poll()` never waits,
  and hands each window its own share of the renderer.
- `LiveView::present` draws only when the frame or the look (theme, size,
  quality) changed, at most 15 fps on `Quality::Battery`, at an idle rate
  when the caller says the creature is calm, never while the window is
  covered. `current_quality` turns Battery on in Low Power Mode.
- `HitTest::hits` says whether a point is on the model as drawn (skinned on
  the CPU, exact); `follow_pointer` makes the window take the mouse only
  there, so clicks elsewhere reach the apps below.
- `Offscreen` and `render_rgba` render without a window, for PNGs and tests.
  Tests that need a GPU skip when there is none.
- The look (light directions and colours) is set in `uniforms::globals`,
  the shadow's tint in `shaders/post.metal` (`SHADOW`); the lighting itself
  is `model_fragment` in `shaders/model.metal`.

## Pitfalls

- objc2 panics when it registers a class whose ivars are aligned past 8
  bytes, and a `glam::Mat4` is aligned to 16: put a `LiveView` or a `Frame`
  in an ivar inside a `Box`. `src/ui/class_tests.rs` registers every class
  in `cargo test`, so a launch never finds out first.
- The structs the shaders read are whole `float4`s, mirrored field for field
  in `shaders/common.metal` (`uniforms::tests` checks the sizes).
- Keep `update_into` allocation free when adding to it: the test above
  counts.
- Past 64 skin joints the skin no longer fits `setVertexBytes`: the
  renderer writes it into `SKIN_BUFFERS` buffers in turn, so keep at most
  two frames in flight per renderer (`LiveView` does).

## Next: a 3D cat

What it would take, in its own PR since it changes the core:

1. A model of the cat whose clips are named after what the behaviour does
   (`Look`: walk, sleep, held, fall…), with `socket_*` empties where the
   bubble and the props go.
2. An opt-in `Style::Model` next to Pixel Art and Classic.
3. In `CatView`, a boxed `LiveView` drawing the animator's frame on the
   tick, the `Look` mapped to clip names, `follow_pointer` for the clicks.
   `creature::frame::Frame` and `creature::pose::Pose` share their names
   with `sprites::Frame` and `sprites::Pose`: import them by path there.
4. The GLB bundled by `scripts/bundle.sh`.
